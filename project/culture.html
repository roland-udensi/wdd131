Identity & Heritage

Igbo Culture

Language, values, family, community, and history all contribute to the richness of Igbo heritage.

The Igbo Language

Igbo is the language of the Igbo people and is spoken mainly in southeastern Nigeria. The language is an important part of cultural identity and provides a way for people to communicate ideas, stories, experiences, and traditions.

Proverbs, greetings, songs, names, and storytelling can all carry cultural knowledge. Preserving and learning the language helps maintain connections between generations.

Family and Community

Family and community relationships are important parts of traditional Igbo social life. Extended family relationships can connect people across generations and create networks of support and responsibility.

Community gatherings, ceremonies, celebrations, and shared responsibilities provide opportunities for people to work together and maintain relationships.

Cultural Values

Respect

Respect for elders, family members, guests, and other members of the community is an important cultural value.

Community

Cooperation and shared responsibility help communities support their members and accomplish common goals.

Hospitality

Welcoming guests and sharing food are important expressions of friendship and community.

Hard Work

Work, responsibility, and determination have long been valued qualities within many Igbo communities.

Preserving Heritage

Cultural heritage is preserved when knowledge, language, stories, practices, and values are passed from one generation to another. Families, communities, schools, cultural organizations, and individuals can all contribute to this process.

Learning about Igbo heritage is one way to appreciate the past while helping cultural knowledge remain meaningful for future generations.

Celebrating Culture, Preserving Traditions

Igbo heritage is more than individual traditions. It is a living connection between people, places, language, history, family, and community.
